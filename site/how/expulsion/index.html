How things work

How expulsion works

Each chamber of Congress can remove one of its own members from office by expelling them. Expulsion takes a two-thirds vote, a higher bar than the chamber's other forms of discipline.

The Constitution

Article I, Section 5, Clause 2 of the Constitution reads:

"Each House may determine the Rules of its Proceedings, punish its Members for disorderly Behaviour, and, with the Concurrence of two thirds, expel a Member."

Each chamber decides on its own members. The House does not need the Senate's agreement to expel a representative, and the Senate does not need the House's to expel a senator.

The vote

A member is expelled by adopting a resolution, usually titled "Expelling…" or "Providing for the expulsion of…". In the House, the question on it is recorded as "On Agreeing to the Resolution", the same wording used for most other House resolutions. This site identifies an expulsion vote by the resolution's title, and its vote page links here.

Two-thirds is counted of the members present and voting, not of the chamber's full membership. On this site, "Yeas needed" for a two-thirds vote is two-thirds of the Yea and Nay votes cast. Members who vote Present or do not vote are not counted.

Other motions can be offered on an expulsion resolution, such as a motion to table it or refer it to a committee. Those motions need a simple majority. If one is agreed to, the resolution is set aside or sent to committee without a vote on expelling the member.

Censure and reprimand

The same clause lets each chamber "punish its Members for disorderly Behaviour" short of expulsion. Censure and reprimand are adopted by a simple majority vote, and neither removes a member from office.

Censure
A formal statement of disapproval, adopted as a resolution. Both the House and the Senate have censured members.
Reprimand
A House sanction considered less severe than censure. In modern practice, the House Committee on Ethics recommends a reprimand or censure by submitting a resolution with a report to the full House.

How often it has happened

House
Six members have been expelled: three in 1861, for supporting the Confederacy during the Civil War, and one each in 1980, 2002 and 2023.
Senate
Fifteen senators have been expelled since 1789: one in 1797, and fourteen in 1861 and 1862, for supporting the Confederacy during the Civil War.
