About

About & methodology

Yeas & Nays publishes every recorded vote in the U.S. House and Senate, taken from the official record and shown without commentary. It does not endorse candidates, parties or positions.

Data sources

Every vote and member record comes from a public, official or widely used source. Each vote page links to its original record.

House roll call votes
Office of the Clerk, U.S. House of Representatives, clerk.house.gov
Senate roll call votes
United States Senate, senate.gov
Members: names, parties, states, districts and terms
congress-legislators, an open dataset maintained by the @unitedstates project, github.com/unitedstates/congress-legislators
Members: official links
Each current member's official website, contact form, Washington office and phone, and official social media accounts, from the same congress-legislators dataset. Profiles show only what it lists; it does not list Bluesky accounts.
Bills: sponsors, summaries and text
Each bill's sponsor (the member who introduced it), introduced date, number of cosponsors and latest action, and the list of bills each member introduced, come from congress.gov. Summaries are written by the Congressional Research Service, Library of Congress, and shown exactly as published, labeled with the version of the bill they describe. Not every measure has one. Each vote page links to the measure's full text on Congress.gov.
Floor schedule ("Coming up")
House: the Majority Leader's weekly floor schedule, docs.house.gov, which lists measures that may be considered that week. Senate: cloture motions filed and not yet voted on, from the Senate's daily floor activity, and the time the Senate next convenes, from senate.gov. Neither is a promise: items are often moved or dropped. See the floor schedule.
Portraits
Official congressional portraits collected by the @unitedstates project, github.com/unitedstates/images. Members without a portrait show their initials.
Updates
The site checks for new votes every hour.

Definitions

Recorded vote
A vote in which each member's position is recorded by name. Voice votes and unanimous consent are not included, because no individual positions are recorded.
Yea, Nay, Not voting
Positions as recorded. The House records some votes as Aye and No; they appear here as recorded and count the same as Yea and Nay. Present is shown as Present and counts toward neither side.
Party majority
The position, Yea or Nay, taken by more than half of a party's members who voted Yea or Nay on that roll call. If a party splits evenly, it has no majority on that vote.
Voted against party majority
A member who voted Yea or Nay opposite their party's majority. Not voting and Present never count as voting against.
Independents
Independents are grouped with the party they caucus with when party majorities are calculated. They are still labeled I everywhere on the site.
Missed votes
Roll calls marked Not voting, as a share of all roll calls held in the member's chamber while they were in office this Congress.
Voted with party majority
The share of a member's Yea and Nay votes that matched their party's majority. Votes where the party had no majority are left out.

How votes work

Many recorded votes are not on whether to pass a measure. They decide how the chamber handles it: whether to take it up, end debate, change it, or send it to a committee. Each vote page states the official question and, under the result, what that kind of vote decides. The result always refers to that question. For example, "Motion to Refer: Passed" means the motion to send the measure to a committee was agreed to, not that the measure itself passed.

Some motions, such as motions to refer or table, are offered while a measure is pending on the floor. If one is agreed to, the measure is set aside or sent to committee without a vote on the measure itself.

How parties and votes are shown

  • Parties are written as text (D, I, R) and always listed alphabetically.
  • Every party gets the same style and emphasis. The site uses no party colors.
  • Positions are always written out. In charts, Yea is solid, Nay is striped and Not voting is open, so they read the same in black and white.
